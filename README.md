# Reduced Genetic Diversity of Key Fertility and Vector Competency Related Genes in Anopheles gambiae s.l. Across Sub-Saharan Africa

Welcome to the GitHub repository accompanying our manuscript:

Title: Reduced Genetic Diversity of Key Fertility and Vector Competency Related Genes in Anopheles gambiae s.l. Across Sub-Saharan Africa

Authors: ​​Fatoumata Seck, Mouhamadou Fadel Diop, Karim Mané, Amadou Diallo, Idrissa Dieng, Moussa Namountougou, Abdoulaye Diabate, Alfred Amambua-Ngwa, Ibrahima Dia, Benoit Sessinou Assogba
Corresponding Author: Benoit S Assogba, Sessinou-Benoit.Assogba@lshtm.ac.uk

## Author summary
In the pursuit of malaria elimination, insecticide resistance poses a significant challenge to vector control efforts. We explored genetic studies to contribute to the development for alternative anti-vector tools, such as small molecule inhibitors of genes implicated in malaria vector fertility and susceptibility to Plasmodium. A comprehensive understanding of the genetic diversity of these critical genes is crucial for successfully implementing this approach in natural vector populations. We identified four promising target genes through a literature review: MISO, Vg, Lp, and HPX15. The analysis of whole-genomic SNP data from the Ag1000G project revealed significant conservation of these genes within Sub-Saharan African populations of Anopheles gambiae s.l., with the highest divergence observed in An. arabiensis populations. Interestingly, MISO showed less structuration compared to other genes. The low nucleotide diversity and heterozygosity within populations suggests purifying selection and low genetic diversity. This essential information support further exploration of MISO, Vg, Lp, and HPX15 genes as putative targets for development of small molecule inhibitors, towards innovating efforts in vector population control and reducing global malaria transmission.
